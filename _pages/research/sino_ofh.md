## Engineering problem

Fuel cells and their power converters release heat into the compartment that houses them. Whether ventilation can hold that room at an acceptable temperature depends on the enclosure construction and insulation, the equipment surface temperature, the airflow available and, critically, the outside climate. A design that works in the North Sea may not work in a hot region.

## Objective and scope

Predict the thermal behaviour of a fuel-cell engine room under varying marine operating conditions, within the project **Safe Installation and Operation of a Fuel Cell System and Hybrid Powertrain in Offshore CTV (SinO-OFH)**. I led Work Package 4.1 (2024). The scope was a room-level thermal model and scenario study. It did not include measurements on a vessel.

## My contribution

As work-package lead I captured the requirements, built the room-temperature model, ran sensitivity studies, and updated the simulations as project specifications were revised.

## Method

The model represents the thermal capacity of the room air, heat transfer through the walls and door, heat input from the equipment surfaces and fan-driven air exchange, with the fan switched against a room-temperature reference. Sensitivity studies on enclosure thermal resistance and fan capacity established how the model responds, before it was run with updated enclosure specifications for several equipment surface temperatures and ambient climates.

## Results and evidence

The scenario study showed that the same enclosure and control target lead to very different ventilation duty in different climates: cool conditions needed little or no forced ventilation, whereas in hot climates ambient-air ventilation alone could not hold the room at the reference temperature. The result made enclosure construction, insulation and the climate envelope design inputs alongside the equipment heat-source specification. Specifications and numerical results are documented in internal project reports and are not reproduced here.

## Limitations

- Model-based predictions that depend on the assumed construction, insulation, heat-transfer and airflow parameters.
- The equipment surface temperature is an input to the room model; the link to the fuel-cell heat calculated in the [3 MW powertrain study](/research/3mw-marine-fuel-cell/) was not modelled as a coupled system.
