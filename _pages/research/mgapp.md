Energy management decides how the sources and storage in a microgrid share demand over time, and its consequences reach beyond the electrical system to fuel use, equipment sizing and heat. My projects in this area connect equipment and load models to operating strategies for a marine fuel-cell and battery powertrain, followed by evaluation of the equipment's thermal environment. I led both projects.

<h2 id="3-mw-marine-fuel-cell-hybrid-powertrain">3 MW Marine Fuel-Cell Project</h2>

In 2023 I led the 3 MW marine fuel-cell hybrid powertrain project, covering system architecture, MATLAB/Simulink modelling and a central rule-based energy management strategy. The study compared battery sizing and operating policies over HST and BMT vessel duty cycles, with results for electrical energy use, component states and fuel-cell heat generation.

[Read the 3 MW marine powertrain study](/research/3mw-marine-fuel-cell/)

<h2 id="sino-ofh-wp41-thermal-evaluation">Thermal Evaluation</h2>

In 2024 I led the thermal evaluation that followed. It developed an engine-room temperature and ventilation model, examined enclosure and fan sensitivity, and compared 45 °C and 60 °C equipment surface temperatures under North Sea, Persian Gulf and Singapore climate scenarios.
