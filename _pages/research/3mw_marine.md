I led the **3 MW marine fuel-cell hybrid powertrain project in 2023**, connecting vessel requirements and system architecture to MATLAB/Simulink component models and a central rule-based energy management strategy, and comparing battery sizing and dispatch policies over two vessel duty cycles.

<h2 id="overview">Matching a Hybrid Powertrain to a Day at Sea</h2>

Offshore vessels alternate between high-power transit, manoeuvring, intermittent work and idle periods. In a fuel-cell and battery hybrid powertrain, the fuel cells supply steady power efficiently while the batteries cover periods when the fuel cells are off and parts of the demand assigned to them. The powertrain must meet the full duty cycle, including onboard auxiliary loads, and the heat generated by the fuel cells becomes a design load in its own right. Battery size, dispatch policy and thermal management therefore have to be assessed together against realistic working days.

The released model represents two propulsion engines with nine cabinets per engine and five fuel cells per cabinet. A central rule-based controller activates the fuel-cell and battery models against time-varying propulsion and auxiliary demand: fuel cells supply demand while on, and batteries take the assigned demand while the fuel cells are off. Simplified converter representations serve this system-level study.

The scheduling study uses 15-minute intervals over a 24-hour day, starting at 80% battery state of charge. Two duty cycles, **HST and BMT**, represent different working days, with the project's **110 kWh HST** and **280 kWh BMT** battery cases under the revised dispatch rule. Outputs include component voltage and current, battery state of charge, and fuel-cell efficiency, reactant flow and generated heat.

<h2 id="outcomes">Design Workflow</h2>

The project delivered a reusable workflow for comparing duty cycles, battery allocations and operating rules. Because the fuel cells' generated heat is modelled alongside their electrical output, the same workflow provides the heat-load context for the subsequent [thermal evaluation](/research/sino-ofh/).
