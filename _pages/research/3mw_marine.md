<h2 id="overview">Matching a Hybrid Powertrain to a Day at Sea</h2>

Offshore vessels alternate between high-power transit, manoeuvring, intermittent work and idle periods. A fuel-cell and battery powertrain must accommodate the full duty cycle, including onboard auxiliary loads and the heat generated by the fuel cells.

I led the **3MW project in 2023**, connecting vessel requirements and system architecture to MATLAB/Simulink component models and a central rule-based energy management strategy.

Equipment models and duty cycles feed the energy management study.

The **HST and BMT** duty-cycle profiles represent different working days. The model compares fuel-cell output, battery state and heat generation across those schedules. A key finding was that battery sizing depends on the dispatch policy: assigning electric manoeuvring and auxiliary loads to the batteries creates a different energy requirement from reserving them for fuel-cell-off periods.

The released simulations use the project’s **110 kWh HST** and **280 kWh BMT** battery case labels with the revised dispatch rule. These labels describe the source cases rather than inferred vessel-wide installed capacities.

<h2 id="operating-strategy">Component Models and Dispatch Rule</h2>

The released model represents two propulsion engines with nine cabinets per engine and five fuel cells per cabinet. A central rule-based controller activates the fuel-cell and battery models against time-varying propulsion and auxiliary demand. Fuel cells supply demand while on; batteries take the assigned demand while the fuel cells are off.

The final scheduling study uses 15-minute intervals over a 24-hour day and 80% initial battery state of charge. Electrical outputs include component voltage/current and battery SoC; the fuel-cell model also supplies efficiency, reactant-flow and generated-heat traces. Simplified converter representations serve the system-level scheduling study.

The power schedule exposes both the electrical demand and the associated fuel-cell heat load.

HST Case 2 reports **13.721 MWh of fuel-cell electrical energy** and **17.188 MWh of generated heat**. Across the minimum and maximum HST cases, electrical energy ranges from **10.704 to 17.190 MWh**, with heat generation of **12.968–22.623 MWh**. These outputs make thermal management a substantial design consideration alongside electrical dispatch.

Repeated BMT demand changes produce a different fuel-cell and battery operating pattern.

The BMT case reports **13.007 MWh of fuel-cell electrical energy** and **16.603 MWh of heat**. All results are simulations under the documented dispatch and assumed auxiliary-load profile.

<h2 id="outcomes">Design Implications</h2>

The project delivered a reusable modelling workflow for comparing duty cycles, battery allocations and operating rules. It showed why capacity and dispatch must be assessed together, and provided heat-load context for the subsequent [thermal evaluation](/research/sino-ofh/).
