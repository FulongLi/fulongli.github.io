## Engineering problem

A solid-state transformer (SST) replaces a line-frequency transformer with power electronics and a high-frequency isolation stage. At a 10 kV input this requires either many series-connected converter cells or higher-voltage devices. The availability and maturity of 3.3 kV SiC MOSFETs therefore shapes the cell count, the isolation-stage design and the control complexity of a medium-voltage SST.

## Objective and scope

Assess the state of the art in high-voltage SiC devices and in 10 kV / 500 kW SST architectures for the **ASSiST** (Advanced SiC-based Solid State Transformer) project at CSA Catapult. The scope was a technology and architecture assessment. It did not include building an SST demonstrator.

## My contribution

- Reviewed 3.3 kV SiC device technology.
- Reviewed 10 kV / 500 kW SST architectures: where each topology originated, its converter stages, control strategies and application scenarios.
- Delivered the associated project milestones.
- Separately, carried out H3TRB [reliability testing](/research/reliability_testing/) of SiC modules for the same project.

## Results and evidence

The deliverables were review reports for the project, which are internal. No SST hardware results exist from this work, and none are claimed.
