Microgrid control methods are only convincing once they run on real converters, batteries and loads. I built low-voltage DC microgrid testbeds at Aston and Loughborough to test converter control, energy storage and system operation together, and worked with an existing larger platform during visiting research at Zhejiang University.

## Aston University: Modular DC Microgrid Testbed

A laboratory microgrid in which four 250 W interface converters connect a PV emulator, two battery banks and a grid emulator to a common DC bus. It carried the experiments of my PhD.

[Read about the Aston testbed](/research/experimental-platforms/aston/)

## Loughborough University: Low-Voltage DC Microgrid

A modular test bench developed through successive interface-converter prototypes, reaching 500 W per converter in its second version.

[Read about the Loughborough testbed](/research/experimental-platforms/loughborough/)

## Zhejiang University: 50 kW DC Microgrid Platform

During visiting research I worked with an existing 50 kW DC microgrid platform, including its three-phase voltage-source converter interface.

[Read about the Zhejiang platform](/research/experimental-platforms/zhejiang/)
