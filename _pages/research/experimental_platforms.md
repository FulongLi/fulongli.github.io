Microgrid control methods are only convincing once they run on real converters, batteries and loads. Low-voltage DC microgrid testbeds were built at Aston and Loughborough to test converter control, energy storage and system operation together, and a larger 50 kW test bench was operated during a research secondment at Zhejiang University.

## Aston University: Modular DC Microgrid Testbed

A laboratory microgrid in which four 250 W interface converters connect a PV emulator, two battery banks and a grid emulator to a common DC bus. It carried the PhD experiments.

[Read about the Aston testbed](/research/experimental-platforms/aston/)

## Loughborough University: Low-Voltage DC Microgrid

A modular test bench developed through successive interface-converter prototypes, reaching 500 W per converter in its second version.

[Read about the Loughborough testbed](/research/experimental-platforms/loughborough/)

## Zhejiang University: 50 kW DC Microgrid Test Bench

A two-month secondment in 2018 covered the operation of a 50 kW DC microgrid test bench: its converter protection, its hierarchical control with passivity-based stabilisation, and the control of its three-phase AC–DC interface converters.

[Read about the Zhejiang test bench](/research/experimental-platforms/zhejiang/)
